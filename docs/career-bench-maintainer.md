# Career Bench Maintainer — role description

**Copy this into wherever the e-board keeps role descriptions.** It lives in the repo so it doesn't get lost, but it belongs attached to the position, not to this file and not to a person.

---

## Why this role exists

A link-heavy site with no named owner is dead in eighteen months. The contribution model alone won't save it — someone has to press merge.

The Career Bench is designed so that maintenance is small and distributed: resources are pooled at the domain rather than per skill, every type is slot-capped, and stale entries surface themselves with a pre-filled issue. That design makes the job survivable. It does not make the job optional.

## The role

**Career Bench maintainer** — an e-board or committee position, attached to the role rather than to whoever currently holds it.

Three responsibilities:

1. **Merge tile PRs.** Check the tile against the bar in [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) — mainly that `first_move` is a physical action and `failure_mode` is something real. Send back anything with "research X" as a first move.

2. **Run a verification pass when greyed resources exceed a threshold.** Resources render greyed past twelve months. When roughly a quarter of a domain's pool has gone grey, open the issues and work through them, or put it to the club as a batch of Tier-1 items people can claim.

3. **Refresh Prove windows before each semester's CFP and registration season.** Competition registration dates, CFP windows, and certification prices move. This is the part that cannot be a printed handout, and it's the part that goes stale fastest and most visibly. Once per semester, before the season, is enough.

## Time cost

Realistically a couple of hours a semester, plus PR review as it arrives. The verification pass is the only thing that clusters, and it's designed to be handed out rather than absorbed.

## What the maintainer does *not* decide alone

- **Adding university systems as targets.** They're excluded by policy, not by oversight. Changing that is an e-board conversation, not a merge.
- **Removing the Tier-0 exclusion.** The gatekeeper ladder is the tool's main argument; Tier 0 not counting as proof is the load-bearing part of it.

## Club-run gates

Two Tier-1 and Tier-2 gates in the tool are ones NUSEC operates, and they only stay real if someone runs them:

- **`PRV-CLUBBLOG`** — the NUSEC blog, *with genuine editorial review*. A post nobody could have rejected is Tier 0. The edit round is what makes it proof.
- **`PRV-LIGHTNING`** — lightning talk slots that are **selected, not first-come**. The selection is the gate.

These are the strongest argument for NUSEC owning this rather than it being a generic careers site: the club can manufacture proof opportunities for its members that don't otherwise exist. If nobody runs them, two tiles in the tool become false.

Whether the maintainer runs these or hands them to whoever owns comms and meetings is a club call — but someone has to own them, and it should be written down next to this.
